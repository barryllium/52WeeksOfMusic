I arrived at SZA for a few reasons. Originally, she was on my list because she has two albums on *Rolling Stone*'s 2023 "The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time" list. Recently, though, I found out that this is also the favorite artist of one of Forest's friends at school. And since it is well known that my most exceptional skill is being in touch with the youth of America<sup>1</sup>, I knew I needed to start listening. It did not go how I expected.

- *Z* (EP, 2014)
- *Ctrl* (2017)
- "All the Stars" (2018)
- *SOS* (2022)
- "luther" (2024)

I was certain this was going to be a slam dunk. I was going to listen and love SZA. I knew two of her influences were Lauryn Hill (I loved the Fugees back in the day) and Imogen Heap (*Details* by Frou Frou is high on my list of albums that I revisit often). But I fired up *Ctrl*<sup>2</sup>, and my reaction after finishing was that this was good, but I was not super into it. More than anything, I was confused.

Here's the thing - SZA can sing *very well*. She has a wide range, with great control as she moves up and down the scale, ripping off some amazing runs, and delivering some impressive melodic rap at times (at one point she squeezed so many syllables into a few seconds it made me think of Daveed Diggs in *Hamilton*). Her falsetto caught me off guard in all the best ways on multiple occasions. I felt like I *should* love her music - and yet I just wasn't there.

Confusing me even more was that I also listened to *Ask Rufus* by Rufus featuring Chaka Khan. SZA and Chaka Khan are not dissimilar artists: both wander off and onto the melody, bending and stretching it as they dance through a song vocally. And I was really into *Ask Rufus*. So, again, I kept asking myself, why was I not getting into SZA?

Listening to *Z* helped, as I connected to more of her music. Two songs in particular grabbed me, the first being "Julia". The programming and synth highlighted every layer of her vocal performance, and the beat was contagious. Didn't matter if she was delivering from the softer part of her range, or if she was putting more power behind it, I was into "Julia". Later in the album, "Shattered Ring" wasted no time grabbing me. Once the guitar comes in, it has my attention and I'm hooked. The song builds up in the chorus, and everything that is happening is working for me. Her tone, the music ebbing and flowing, the elements all complement each other.

There are two singles that I listened to, both of which are actually Kendrick Lamar songs. Now, to be clear, I can't properly condone working with Kendrick Lamar, nor listening to him. To attempt to be fair, both "All the Stars" and "luther" have some fantastic beats, and I think I would love them if one thing were different: Kendrick didn't sing on them. His nasal tone is anathema to me, I can't stand it, and I can't not hear it. Sometimes it's there less than other times, but it's always there, and it's like nails on a chalkboard to me. With that said, SZA sounds great on both tracks; the strings on "All the Stars" sound amazing and work really well with her voice, and "luther" also brings the magic of a strings backing (not to mention the acoustic guitar work and a pretty dope beat).

*SOS* was where things clicked for me in a few directions, though. The first thing clicked for me while listening to "Kill Bill". Sometimes, when SZA is singing in more of a mellow tone, I hear something with her voice that doesn't work for me. The only way I can think to describe it is that it sounds like the audio version of putting a blur effect on a photo. It's not always there, but when it is, I find myself wanting her to sing like she does the rest of the time. Whenever she's melodically rapping, or when she puts her power into a note, or when she hits her delightful falsetto - those moments I'm enjoying her voice. But those "blur" moments, I'm just not loving those.

But, luckily, the back half of *SOS* exists - particularly a four-song stretch that includes "F2F", "Nobody Gets Me", and "Special". "F2F" has me from the 15-second mark when the music picks up, and I'm there for good when it fully kicks in for the chorus. More so, the strength of her voice works excellently with the music. And then, when we are back in the verse, she puts the right amount of feeling in her performance to show the complicated emotions behind the song.

"Nobody Gets Me", ironically, is one of the songs where I got her the most. Her delivery provides emotional weight throughout the song, and the acoustic guitar really sets the tone and feel that her voice lives in. I love her control in the chorus, and her power leading into her falsetto might be the peak vocal moment for me (which, despite things I've said, had many impressive moments).

"Special" doesn't hit the same highs, but she gives a moving performance as the music builds up and settles down behind her. All of her vocal layers sound great together, adding even more depth to the song.

As I reach the end of this, I still don't know exactly what I think about SZA. What I like about her, I genuinely like. And it's easy to see how someone else could like her even more - which brings me to the conclusion that she's an excellent singer who just isn't quite for me. And I'm OK with that.

- Favorite album: This barely goes to *SOS* over *Z* (and not because *Z* is "only" an EP). That four-song stretch in the back half was my favorite stretch on any of her albums. There are a few misses on the album for me, but there is enough good with a bit of great on it to give it the nod.
- Favorite song: This is tougher. My initial inclination is "Julia", because the music is so good. But I'm going to give it to "Nobody Gets Me" because of the strength of her vocal performance there. She hits the right note at every point in this song, rising and falling, pushing and pulling back at all the right times. Engaging and emotional all the way through.
