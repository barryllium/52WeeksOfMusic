Trampled by Turtles came to me via my friend Rob. Years ago, I saw him wearing a hat with the band's name on it, and I asked him what that was (assuming it was somehow a Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles reference). So I knew at some point I was going to listen to them during this journey, and I was certain I was going to love them, because how could I not love Rob's favorite band?

- *Songs from a Ghost Town* (2004)
- *Blue Sky and the Devil* (2005)
- "Rich/Poor" (2007)
- *Duluth* (2008)
- *Palomino* (2010)
- "Where Is My Mind?" (2011) <small>second playthrough only</small>
- *Stars and Satellites* (2012)
- *Wild Animals* (2014)
- "The Middle" (2018)
- "Fake Plastic Trees" (2019) <small>second playthrough only</small>
- *Alpenglow* (2022)
  - First playthrough: "It's So Hard to Hold On"
  - Second playthrough: Full album

One song in, I knew I was in trouble. You see, before this week, I would have told you that of all the instruments in the world, the banjo was my least favorite to be featured in a song<sup>1</sup>. Have it as an accent? Yeah, that's great. As part of the rhythm, but melding into other instruments, I'm OK with that. But make it the thing you are featuring in the song? No thanks. Why, it was only [last week](https://barrylliumlabs.com/music/post.html?file=NoahKahan.md) that I was talking about the banjo, and how it can work for me. Apparently, Trampled by Turtles did not become time travelers and read my words before recording their albums from two decades ago. There was a lot of banjo, a lot of mandolin, and I was going to have to deal with it.

I do need to pause and be a bit fair here, though. These are very impressive musicians; they all play their respective instruments *extremely* well. I found myself often tapping my toes to the music, as the rhythm they were creating was solid at worst, infectious at best. But the tone, the tone of the instruments they play is just not what I look for. Similar to a comment I made last week about Mumford & Sons, if you replaced the banjo with a drummer, I think I would have been into it. The best way I can say how I felt about the first few albums is something I typed in my notes halfway through *Songs from a Ghost Town*: this is the most I have ever liked music that I don't like.

But then we get to *Duluth*. From the start of "November", I was assaulted by the music - it was the musical equivalent of a backyard football game, when the QB says "everyone go long". I couldn't feel a plan to the music, as if everyone was trying to be the lead at all times. A few songs into the album, the feeling of the music crystallized for me - I felt like I was listening to a panic attack. I was being hit with sound from every direction, full chaos, and there was nothing I could do to filter anything out from it. Even the addition of the fiddle as a permanent fixture in their songs, which is a positive in the entire arc of their discography, unfortunately was one more data point in all the sounds crashing into my ears. At this point, Trampled by Turtles was really challenging my friendship with Rob, as this is his favorite album, and it is my least.

Things start to take a turn in *Palomino*. The overall [firehose](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pgPgsvxxxKE&t=26s) of instrumentation starts to settle down, the fiddle gets to stand out a little more, the banjo settles more into the rhythm instead of the lead, and the mandolin is used more strategically (an accent here, part of the rhythm there, and sparingly in the lead). Hope springs as I start to notice more songs that worked for me, such as "Victory" and "Separate" - and, of course, the harmonica won me over immediately in "Bloodshot Eyes". But this album is about "Again" for me. There is some real heart behind this song - that I'm allowed to feel because the music isn't coming at me en masse. The fiddle kills it when it's accenting everything else, and the acoustic guitar/bass provide an excellent foundation. It was the first song all week that gave me a taste of the potential of what this band could deliver.

Now we'll do a quick aside, to the two covers I listened to. My initial reaction to "Where Is My Mind?" was "what have they done to my boy?". But once I got some separation from my feelings on the original, I started to like it. The bass and fiddle sound good, and the mandolin oddly fits the song well. The vocals lack the character of Black Francis, but it was still a fun remix. "Fake Plastic Trees" was a step better, though. There was still the drastic vocal difference that I felt missing from the original, but they still managed to capture and respect the spirit of it. The banjo, fiddle, and cello are quite effective, and the mandolin is really cool. They are no Radiohead, but I'd happily listen to this cover any day.

Back to the albums, where *Stars and Satellites* was a revelation. This wasn't just an album that I liked in comparison to everything else; this was an album I really liked, period. The first two tracks alone made the week. "Midnight on the Interstate" has the correct amount of everything (banjo, fiddle, mandolin) to elevate the song, and the moment about one minute in when the fiddle and bass come in is a great move, bringing the song together. One of the better performances of the week. "Alone" ticks so many boxes for me - a restrained feel with emotional depth behind the vocal performance, and the bass coming in around the 1.5-minute mark (closely followed by the fiddle) is damn near perfect, and the buildup to the final third is awesome on its own. And the cello - which I think starts making an appearance on this album, later becoming a permanent band member - is the right touch at all the right times.

There are a few songs on the album that don't quite hit for me, with "Sorry" having too much twang for my tastes, and "Don't Look Down" just having too much mandolin (and its high tone) to click for me. But the rest of the album continues strong. "High Water" conveys its sadness well, the music allowing for a depth not present on previous albums. "Risk" slightly wins me over with the banjo, shockingly. "Widower's Heart" and "Beautiful" both bring a lot to the table, and help to round out what ends up being a formidable album.

*Wild Animals* has its moments, but doesn't live up to the highs of *Stars and Satellites*. It starts out strong, with "Wild Animals" and "Hollow" each having a distinctive feel crafted by solid vocal performances that worked well with arrangements built to define an atmospheric experience for the song. "Wild Animals" does it with a restrained instrumental approach and almost ethereal background vocals; "Hollow" does it with acoustic guitar/banjo/mandolin creating a pleasing rhythm and the fiddle/background vocals branching off of the rhythm in multiple directions. From there, though, the album settles into a several-song stretch that didn't hit for me, for a variety of reasons, before picking itself back up with "Ghosts" and "Lucy". "Ghosts" is a bit haunting (fittingly), with the fiddle and bass combining with background vocals to set that effect. "Lucy" wins me over with the bass and percussion, and the fiddle keeps me there every time it makes an appearance. And the album ends on a high note with my favorite song on the album, "Winners". It starts out restrained but fills out gradually, gaining depth along the way. But while I found myself appreciating a handful of songs on this album, I didn't find myself truly getting into it like the previous album.

Finally, we arrive at *Alpenglow* - and honestly, I feel tricked. Both times through I loved "It's So Hard to Hold On"; it was one of my favorite songs of the week. And from there... the album kinda fades out for me. There were moments here and there, but overall I was unable to engage with it at all. It felt lazy - not to say it was lazily made, but it lacked an energy that made me want to care about it.

There is an idea that has been running through my mind for many reasons lately, and that's that you have to be bad at something before you can be good at it. To a degree, that sums up a lot of how I feel this week. I'm not trying to say that the band was ever bad - in fact, if you love bluegrass (and specifically if you enjoy the banjo and mandolin), I have the feeling that you would love Trampled by Turtles right out of the gate and never look back. For me, though, I heard - and more so felt - the growth of a band. They started out with some raw talent. Eventually they began to build up the band, and, more so, to find their path forward. At first, everyone played their hearts out and made a song out of it; over time, they honed the arrangements, learned restraint, added in a few key instruments (fiddle and cello), and that culminated in a downright achievement with *Stars and Satellites*. While I don't see myself coming back to their entire catalog, I will revisit that album. I just hope we can still be friends, Rob 😉

- Favorite Album: I think it's obvious - *Stars and Satellites*. Head and shoulders above anything else I listened to from them, and one of the better albums I've heard recently.
- Favorite Song: There are a few candidates, but it comes down to "Alone" for me. I could narrow it down to the moments when the bass and then the fiddle come in. Everything comes together in this song, and it's easily the most I liked the banjo all week.
