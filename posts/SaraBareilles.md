Once upon a time, there was a Wii U game named *The Voice: I Want You*. One of the tracks you could sing was "Brave" by Sara Bareilles - and my oldest could sing the hell out of that song. Not only was this my introduction to Sara Bareilles, but it was also when I realized I was never going to beat anyone in my family in this game.

Fast forward several years, and my wife started listening to the soundtrack for *Waitress*. It's a fun show with good music, and essentially led to my wife recommending her this week.

- *Little Voice* (2007)
- *Kaleidoscope Heart* (2010)
- *The Blessed Unrest* (2013)
- *What's Inside: Songs from Waitress* (2015)
- *Amidst the Chaos* (2019)
- *Good Grief* (2026) <small>second playthrough only</small>

Right from the start, I get into "Love Song" on *Little Voice*. This is a song I've heard before, with a great beat and fantastic vocals. And it's not just the lead vocals - it's the layered background vocals. Throughout most of her discography, she's singing multiple tracks, harmonizing with herself or just backing, and her range is often on showcase. With "Love Song", the stage is set for a really strong album.

"Between the Lines", "City", and "Gravity" are the songs that take this album to the next level, though (I will also give a nod to "Come Round Soon", which has a fun beat that reminded me of "Virtual Insanity" by Jamiroquai in all the right ways). "Between the Lines" has a beautiful piano opening, and the stripped-down arrangement gets a wonderful lift when the bass and percussion come in - elevating the song up without changing the feel or overpowering her voice. The emotion in her vocal performance is almost entrancing - and this was the first of many songs where her mastery made me think of Joni Mitchell. I don't make that comparison lightly - her control, and the way she travels through her range with such effortless ease, are at such a high degree that I was repeatedly reminded of Joni's pure skill in those departments.

"City" offered a different mood - it has a BIG feel, and I adore the cello in it; the cello adds a strong dynamic that, combined with the piano, really makes the song stand out. There are some great musical swells throughout the song, and she delivers a masterclass in how to go in and out of your falsetto.

Ah, but then we get to "Gravity". Her voice, a piano, and a cello? Yes, please. It's a simply arranged song that perfectly pulls off what it's trying to do. The song itself has a gravity to it, pulling me constantly in. Her delivery of the chorus is a marvelous journey, and again her control is off-the-charts good. The bridge brings in the strings, which creates an excellent tone change, giving the song a vibrant lift that rises into her vocal high before fading back down for a quiet, lovely finish. It's a superb, stunningly emotional ride.

I won't say too much about *Kaleidoscope Heart* - it was the weakest of her albums for me. That doesn't mean it was bad - it just didn't stand quite as high as her previous and future work. "Breathe Again" is the standout work from the album, though - strong emotion, beautiful vocal layering accompanied by an arrangement that keeps building itself up wonderfully to the 1:40 mark when the bass comes in and the piano changes key. And that final chorus, when most of the music drops out - only to then surge back in with a heightened vocal performance... it's so powerful. It manages to draw me in even more to a song that already has its grasp on me.

*The Blessed Unrest* brings us right back to some top-notch Sara Bareilles with the aforementioned "Brave". The drums and piano are supreme with each other here, the vocals are on point, and the cello adds a new, distinct layer for the final third. I love the energy of the song. This album features the one song all week that I didn't like - "Satellite Call". There is a weird vocal effect going on with her voice, and it keeps undercutting the big stage that the piano and percussion set. But don't worry - there are a lot of other really good songs like "Manhattan", "Eden" (hot Prince vibes on this one), and two more of my favorite songs of the week: "Cassiopeia" and "I Choose You".

The strings - and to a slightly lesser degree, the synth/programming - are the highlight of "Cassiopeia". The beat gets into my bones, and I feel the song throughout my entire body; the chorus even manages to take this to a new level beyond the verses. And then we get to "I Choose You" - which, if you've been reading me for the past year+, you know that I love me some love. Sublime vocals from the lead all the way to the background, and damn do the cello and bass work well together. Some fantastic lyrics in there too, lines like "I am not scared of the elements, I am under-prepared, but I am willing" really spoke to me. Quickly passed the "gonna text this song to my wife" test 😍

Next up, we take a major turn into the musical world (though, to be fair, we get hints of this as far back as "King of Anything" on *Kaleidoscope Heart*). I like *Waitress* - it's a good, sweet show with some emotional depth to it. The back half is where this show/album takes off for me, though. Yes, "When He Sees Me" is an early winner: the staccato rhythm is catchy, and the chorus absolutely makes the song. But "You Matter to Me", "She Used to Be Mine", and "Everything Changes" create a tremendous run to essentially close out the album. "You Matter to Me" is beautiful, full stop. The piano and strings are gorgeous in concert with each other. Both vocal performances are excellent, especially the harmonies (or any time they are both singing). There is a moment, though, that defines this whole song for me. When Sara starts singing, the strings come in a little more, and it highlights her voice in a way that makes her sound even better.

Oh, but then we have "She Used to Be Mine". Let's start with some disclaimers here - I've never been a young woman, or a little girl; I've never been pregnant; I've never been a waitress. But this song gets to me **every time**. The idea of looking back at the past versions of myself, the innocence lost, the things that were that are no more - regardless of whether that's a good thing or a bad thing - the speaks to all of those things and more. The piano, her vocals, the slight rises here and there. The EMOTION behind every line. The way she delivers the line "She's imperfect, but she tries" hits me every time, and we are back in our recurring "what songs make Brett cry" segment. And we aren't done, because the song will wreck me a little bit more when she sings "Who'll be reckless, just enough". Her singing is legit magic. Just go listen to it. I'm not crying, you're crying... I'm just gonna go for a second, I have a... um... food in the oven...

OK, where were we. Ah yes, we were clearly about to start talking about *Amidst the Chaos*. The acoustic bass is such a treat throughout this whole album. I notice it on every song, and it always stands out adding a wonderful texture. With that said, this album started off slow for me - the first several songs have a darker feel to them, and I felt like I was missing half her range in them. I ended up feeling like I would appreciate the first half of the album more with a handful more listens, but as it stands now, this album is another one where the back half dominates for me. Starting with "Miss Simone", the rest of the album really works for me - despite the fact that John Legend appears in "A Safe Place to Land" (yes, he's an exceptionally good singer - and no, I just don't like what he brings to the table ever. His voice sounds like constant smiling, which doesn't work when trying to convey any other emotions). But "Orpheus" and "Poetry by Dead Men" alone are worth the price of admission for this album<sup>1</sup>.

"Orpheus" is an arresting combination of acoustic instrumentation, percussion, and a shining vocal performance. The previously mentioned acoustic bass creates a mood that everything lives in, and that gets heightened dramatically by the orchestra that joins in partway through. When those strings arrive, they somehow (once again) elevate her vocals. "Poetry by Dead Men" follows right after this, and her piano playing really evokes the phrase "tickling the ivories". It's like she's dancing across the keys, and it engages me immediately. The transition into the chorus is the thing that truly wins me over on the song. The strings entering - principally the cello - combined with the piano subtly changing tempo, hit hard. There are a few moments when most of the music drops out leaving only the strings, and those are some top-notch moments as well. The emotion in the vocals is the final piece of the puzzle to bring this song to the top on this album.

At the end of all of this, I decided to check out her latest album, *Good Grief*. It's a solid album that I overall liked - but I think it deserves another listen before I really say much. "Hands Off My Body" is a powerful song with even more powerful lyrics and an excellent beat. "Still Crying" was easily my favorite song, though. I'm assuming she's talking about just listening to herself sing "She Used to Be Mine", which makes total sense. Her vocal performance is crazy good, and the vocal layering nails everything it's trying to do. The music builds up and falls back down at many points, which is super engaging. When the piano joins the acoustic guitar in the second half of the song, that layer makes an arresting song even more so.

I always love listening to an artist that I'm somewhat familiar with and realizing I like them even more. She doesn't have a bad album, and has many songs I didn't talk about that are well worth the listen. Her voice and range are amazing, her compositions always complement the vocals well, and there is a depth to her songs that I feel like I've barely tapped. After listening to almost all of her discography, I feel like there's still so much more to hear even just by listening again.

- Favorite album: *Little Voice* takes this for me. Every album is good, and some are borderline great. But *Little Voice* really blew me away the first time I listened to it, and it held up on the next listen. It starts strong, and it ends stronger.
- Favorite song: For as tight a race as it is for my *second favorite song* this week, "She Used to Be Mine" is the clear winner here. That song hits on so many levels, it's kinda unfair.
- Your playlist: Gonna try something new this time. Here's what I'd recommend to anyone wanting to check out Sara Bareilles:
  - *Little Voice*
  - "Breathe Again"
  - "Brave"
  - "Cassiopeia"
  - "I Choose You"
  - "You Matter to Me"
  - "She Used to Be Mine" (no shame if you play this on repeat for a bit)
  - Start *Amidst the Chaos* with "Miss Simone" and play the rest
  - "Still Crying"
  - "Hands Off My Body"

Enjoy, and see you soon after I listen to some SZA
