Since my whole music project started with the Pixies, I thought it only fitting to enter the next phase of the journey with The Breeders, with Kim Deal being the link. This new journey starts with:

- *Pod* (1990)
- *Last Splash* (1993)
- *Title TK* (2002)
- *All Nerve* (2018)

Similarly to the Pixies, it took me a little bit of time to come around to The Breeders. *Pod* started out slow for me, despite "Doe" grabbing me with its guitar intro and "Oh!" feeling a lot like a Pixies song, with the added violin giving it some depth that I appreciated. But there was a discordance to the album - a lot of abrupt stops to rhythms, things that felt unfinished before moving on to the next section of the song, or even the next note. And I *think* I get it - this was all intentional, this is what they were going for - to the point that I started to appreciate it a bit on my second listen. Even on my first listen, though, it's on the tail end where this album shines for me. Around 35 seconds into "Opened", the music kicks in (and it does this again near the end of the song), and this was my first "the music grabbed me" moment for The Breeders. Right after that, we get "Only In 3's", which has a really good pace to it (the bass sets it well), and the vocals and music rise/fall together nicely. The background vocals sound strong, and overall it ends up just having a nice feel to it. Follow that up with the harmonies combining with music that really drives the song forward in "Limehouse", and this is a strong back half to an album.

*Last Splash* is where I first really felt like I understood the band, though. "New Year" is a strong album starter, but it's the iconic intro to "Cannonball" followed by "Invisible Man" that kick this album up into a higher gear. I knew "Cannonball", even though I didn't know it well, and didn't know it was a Breeders song. That intro - it's magic. The bass already sounds awesome, and then the guitars come in. The whole song is super catchy with terrific vocals. "Invisible Man" isn't quite on the level of "Cannonball", but the violin sets it apart and the vocals flow in concert with the music in a way that worked well for me.

The best parts, for me, about *Last Splash*, were the harmonies and the overall consistency. Kim and Kelley Deal (and sometimes Josephine Wiggs) have some really striking harmonies, their voices melding together so well (a benefit of twins, perhaps?). Song after song had moments where their voices together would grab my attention because it was so melodic. Beyond that, outside of "Roi" and its reprise, I liked every song on the album. There were a few standouts after "Cannonball" and "Invisible Man" - "Do You Love Me Now?" and "Drivin' On 9" both had strong vocal presence, with the latter being a good "change of pace" song on the tail end of the album. "I Just Wanna Get Along" has a potent early bass/drums combo, and the guitar fits in expertly whenever it comes in; it's a simple song with simple lyrics delivered well.

While I liked *Title TK* more on my second listen, it was the letdown album for me both times through. A lot of the songs felt too much like the other songs on the album for me, I just wasn't getting enough differentiation to keep me from getting lulled by it as a whole. The two exceptions were early on - "London Song" has an impressive moment early when the percussion and then the vocals come in (with the guitar hopping back in right after that) - great bass, great beat, commanding vocals. And "Off You" was one of my favorite songs of the week. The vocal performance is emotional and still quietly powerful, and the bass/guitars complement that very well while still being assertive and distinct themselves.

Thankfully, The Breeders get back on track with *All Nerve*. I could probably talk about every song on this album, either because it was the one or two songs that I didn't really like ("Wait in the Car" and "Skinhead #2") or because I really liked it. I feel like they took a lot of chances on this album, and almost every "experiment" paid off for me. Yes, they were still bass-forward, with rich harmonies, but they also mixed things up a lot. From the weird vocal arrangement of "MetaGoth" to the music/vocal combo on "Archangel's Thunderbird", the differences are what made these songs work for me.

And then there were the songs that *really* worked for me, namely "All Nerve", "Spacewoman", and "Howl at the Summit". That musical shift 40 seconds into "All Nerve" is killer, and the song does an impressive job of continually establishing different moods that work together. "Spacewoman" has a fantastic buildup to it, and the progression doesn't cease to sound good along the way; plus, it has one of the best vocal performances of any of their songs. And "Howl at the Summit" has a big feel to it, layer upon layer that mix well with all the other layers, it was one of the more masterfully composed songs of the week for me.

For all that I liked, I still can't help but compare The Breeders to the Pixies. I loved the Pixies, and have been happily revisiting them since I finished my first 52 weeks. And while I like The Breeders, they just did not hit that same consistent level for me. Yes, they had some high highs, a few songs that I really liked. But some bit of charisma, some pull, is missing. In some ways, a strength of The Breeders is a weakness as well - the harmonies are so good, so pure, that it keeps some songs from having that rawness that the Pixies pull off so well. That might not be all of it, but that's something that I can't help but feel when I compare - perhaps a comparison that just isn't fair, but it's still there for me.

To be even more unfair to The Breeders, before listening to them (and between my two playthroughs), I've been listening to a lot of great albums from artists that I love, but haven't listened to those specific albums. For instance, I've listened to Queen's *Greatest Hits I & II* hundreds of times, but I've never listened to *A Night at the Opera* before. Imagine that, along with albums by Aerosmith, Tom Petty, Led Zeppelin, Billy Joel, and others - and almost any artist is going to suffer by comparison. So The Breeders will be an interesting one to re-visit down the road someday, to see how I feel about them outside of those comparisons.

- Favorite album: I really thought this was going to be *Last Splash*, but dammit if *All Nerve* just doesn't come through for me just a little bit more. I don't know if it's just that it hits a wider range in what it delivers, or if it's because it follows up as such a huge leap from *Title TK*, but throughout my second listen I found myself liking it more and more.
- Favorite song: Again, this was going to be "Cannonball", and by all rights it probably should be. But "Spacewoman" gets it for me. I'm often mixed on distortion, but the bass/guitars nail it on that front, and the song has such a great feel to it. But damn, "Cannonball" is great, too.

And there we have it, step one on a new journey. Next up, The Taxpayers.
