52 artists in 52 weeks. I didn't quite know what I was getting into, and I still think I got more out of it than I thought I would. I've developed an even deeper love and appreciation for music, and find myself listening to it differently because of it.

After 52 weeks of doing this, it kinda feels like I need to do some sort of ranking. I'm not going to list my favorite songs - that's way too hard. But I will give you my top artists and albums - in the order I listened to them, because I can't really rank them.

* Favorite Artists:
  * Pixies
  * Billie Eilish
  * boygenius
  * David Bowie
  * Rush
  * Muse
* Favorite Albums:
  * *Doolittle* - Pixies
  * *the record* - boygenius
  * *The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars* - David Bowie
  * *Rumours* - Fleetwood Mac
  * *Otis Blue* - Otis Redding
  * *Absolution* - Muse

Does that mean these are the best artists and albums of the last 52 weeks? No - but of all the artists and albums I've listened to, these are the ones I'm most excited to revisit (and, in some cases, check out more of their work). Ask me again in a week, a month, and a year, and I'll probably have some different answers for you.

So, you ask, what now? Again, thank you for asking - I do truly love our back-and-forths. In the immediate future, I'm going to take some breaths. For as much as I loved the experience, it was *a lot*, and some weeks were notably more than others. In fact, here's an idea of what I listened to in the past year:

* 301 albums
* 76 singles
* 78 of the top 500 albums from Rolling Stone's 2023 list

And remember, I listened to almost all of that twice. At an average of 45 minutes per album, that's 451.5 **hours** of music from the albums alone. That time doesn't include singles, doesn't include the handful of albums I listened to a third time, and doesn't account for the many albums that were 1-2 hours long. This was a lot of music over the course of one year. So yes, I'm going to take a little break from "new to me" music for now.

That's not to say I'm not listening to music. In the past week alone, I went to a jazz concert, listened to The Strokes' new album *Reality Awaits* (I liked it - they lean a bit too much into vocal effects, but the music is great and I connected with "Lonely in the Future" and "Falling out of Love" in my first two listens), and also checked out Flea's *Honora* album - a weird bass/trumpet jazz album he put out, that is worth listening to even though it is kind of odd (though I did like "Thinkin Bout You", his take on a Frank Ocean song). So yes, I'm still listening to music, [this is my life now](https://c.tenor.com/E1kFGOQQpJsAAAAd/tenor.gif).

Beyond that, I'm revisiting some of my favorite artists and songs from the past year. In fact, if you use Apple Music, [here's a nice playlist](https://music.apple.com/us/playlist/52-weeks/pl.u-NpXmB8kCLYGal) of my 2-3 favorite songs from each artist. I'm going to enjoy some music and listen to music just for the sake of listening to it.

But that doesn't mean that this blog is going away. I have a long list of artists that didn't make the original 52 cut, plus I've been asking several people I know to give me an artist to look into. The result is I already have over 60 artists on the list. Since I don't plan on doing this one week at a time from here on out, I've got a few years' worth of artists to enjoy getting to know. Beyond that, I plan on trying to listen to most of the rest of Rolling Stone's top 500 albums here and there, some as one-offs, others leading to me covering more artists here<sup>*</sup>.

Above all, there is one gift I gained from this year that I want to pass along, and that's the realization that there are so many ways to love music. There are some songs I loved because the lyrics intrigued my brain. There were other songs that grabbed my heart with some combination of lyrics, vocal performance, and music. There are others that got into my bones and just infected my body in the best possible way. Some songs managed to do multiple of these things. I can't say that I enjoy all music now, but I can say that I understand why anyone loves the music they love, regardless of how I feel about it. I found something to like *every week*, even the weeks where I didn't like most of what I was hearing. Music is everywhere, find what you like, and enjoy the ride. See you in a few weeks.
